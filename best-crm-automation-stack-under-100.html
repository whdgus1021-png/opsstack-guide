The best CRM and automation stack under $100 a month

Three builds for a five-person agency, priced from the vendors' own pages on 26 November 2026. The automation engine is the cheap part; the CRM seats are where $100 goes.

Short answer

For five people, HubSpot Sales Hub Starter at the current $7-per-seat new-customer offer plus Make Core is $44 a month: a shared pipeline, email automation, a workflow engine, and Looker Studio and ClickUp's free plan covering reports and boards.

Swap in n8n Cloud as the engine and pay for three project seats and the same team sits at $83.87, still inside the ceiling. What actually breaks $100 is the CRM: five HubSpot seats at list price are $100 before a single workflow runs.

What does each layer of the stack cost in November 2026?

The stack is three meters, not one: a CRM bills per seat, an automation tool per unit of work, a project tool per seat again. Today the cheapest credible reading of each is $7, $9 and $0, and the most useful is $7, about $22 and $9.

What does a run, a seat and a client actually cost?

Make Core works out at $0.90 per 1,000 credits, n8n Cloud Starter at roughly $8.75 per 1,000 executions, and the balanced build at $10.48 per client a month for eight clients. Those are the three figures worth arguing about at renewal, because each fails differently.

Make's $9 buys 10,000 credits and most module actions cost one credit each. A monthly client report — read a sheet, filter, format, write a row, mail a link — is about six credits per client; a daily three-module task sync across eight clients over 30 days is 720. That is under 800 credits, so credits are not what stops a five-person agency; the free plan's two-scenario cap and 15-minute interval are.

n8n counts executions rather than steps, so its unit price falls as you climb: Starter €8 per 1,000 (≈$8.75), Pro €5 per 1,000 (≈$5.47). Starter caps a single execution at five minutes and five concurrent runs, so a contact export or a big PDF merge cannot run on it at any volume. The 40-minute ceiling on Pro is the real reason to move up, not volume.

Per seat the lean build is $8.80 a person and the balanced one $16.77; per client across eight retainers, $5.50 and $10.48. The CRM's list price is what cannot be absorbed quietly.

Where does the $100 break first?

Seats and the CRM's list price break it long before automation volume does. Three pressure points, in the order a growing agency meets them.

1. The promotion ends. HubSpot's $7 is a new-customer discount against a $20 list tied to an annual commit. At renewal, or the day a sixth seat is added at list, the CRM line goes from $35 to $120 and the stack is over budget with nothing added. If it has to survive three years, model $20 a seat and call $7 a first-year saving of $65 a month.

2. The fifth paid project seat. ClickUp Core at three seats is $27, at five $45. That line alone takes the balanced build to $101.87. Keep the people who only read boards on free view-only guest seats.

3. Usage, eventually. 10,000 Make credits cover about 13 clients on the report above plus the daily sync; hourly polling on more modules runs them out mid-month. Extra credits sell in 1,000 and 10,000 bundles at the plan's fixed rate, so overage on Core is still $0.90 per 1,000 — predictable, unlike a per-task bill that jumps a whole tier.

The trap in the free CRM

HubSpot's free tier is a usable CRM with two users. A five-person agency will import client records first and meet the seat wall when the third person needs access — by then the switch costs more than the subscription. Decide the seat count before the import, not after.

Who should not buy this

Not the $44 build if you are two people — HubSpot free plus Make free is enough and costs nothing; if you need forecasting, sequences or conversation intelligence, which start on Sales Hub Professional at $90 a seat; or if a client contract demands SSO, audit logs or a named support contact.

Not HubSpot Starter at list price if the CRM is mostly a filing cabinet and the pipeline lives in your head. systeme.io at $17 covers 5,000 contacts with unlimited sub-accounts and email sends; at $100 for five HubSpot seats, the CRM is the entire budget.

Not Make if workflows run past 40 minutes or branch heavily — that is n8n's ground. Not n8n Cloud Starter if you need version control, environments or SSO: those start at Business, self-hosted only, €667 a month.

Not any of it if the team is under three. A shared sheet and a scheduled email will do; our reporting guide has that free route, trap included.

How the pieces connect

Connect the CRM first, then one report workflow, then the board. The CRM holds the records every automation reads, so it fixes your field names; the report is the first workflow worth automating because it has a deadline; the board comes last, because built first it becomes a second place where status has to be updated by hand.

FAQ

What is the cheapest CRM and automation stack under $100 a month?

For five people, HubSpot Sales Hub Starter at the $7 new-customer offer ($35 for five seats, annual) plus Make Core at $9 comes to $44, with Looker Studio and ClickUp's free plan covering reports and boards. Every figure was read from the vendor pages on 26 November 2026.

Can a five-person agency run on free plans alone?

Not the CRM: HubSpot's free tier stops at two users. Make's free plan gives 1,000 credits but only two active scenarios and a 15-minute minimum interval. ClickUp Free Forever does hold boards for a team that size.

Is Make or n8n cheaper per run?

They meter different things. Make Core is $0.90 per 1,000 credits and most module actions cost one credit; n8n Starter is about $8.75 per 1,000 executions, but one execution covers a whole multi-step workflow. Long workflows are cheaper on n8n; short, frequent ones are cheaper on Make.

What pushes this stack over $100 a month?

Seats. The fifth paid ClickUp seat adds $18 and takes the balanced build to $101.87; HubSpot returning to its $20 list price adds $65 at five seats. Automation volume binds last: 10,000 Make credits cover roughly 13 clients on a monthly report plus a daily three-step sync.
