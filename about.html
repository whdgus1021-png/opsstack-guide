About OpsStack Guide

A small, independent publication about the software that runs the back office of a studio: automation engines, CRMs, and the glue between them.

Last updated 22 November 2026

What this is

OpsStack Guide publishes comparisons and build guides for the person who owns operations at a three-to-fifty-person agency: the one who set up the automations, pays the software bill, and gets asked why the monthly report was late.

The site is small on purpose. We would rather keep twenty pages with prices we have actually read than two hundred generated from a keyword list. Nothing on this site is produced by scraping another review, and no page is published without at least one number we checked ourselves.

Who writes it

OpsStack Guide is written and maintained by its editors, who build and run these automations in their own work. We do not publish personal biographies or client names, and we do not name the agencies whose workflows informed a comparison. When an article describes an operational failure — a task limit hit mid-delivery, a dashboard showing stale numbers — it is a pattern we have seen or reproduced, not a quotation from a named client.

How prices are checked

  • Primary sources only. Vendor pricing pages, product documentation and — where a vendor publishes one — the legal price catalog. Never another review site's table.
  • The actual toggle. Where a pricing page defaults to annual billing, we record the annual price and note it. Where a promotion replaces the list price, both are recorded and labelled.
  • Dated. Every article carries the date its numbers were read. Prices are re-checked quarterly and immediately after a vendor repackages its plans.
  • Unit economics, not sticker prices. A $19.99 plan that includes 100 units and a $9 plan that includes 10,000 are not in the same category of product; we convert to a per-unit cost so the comparison is honest.

How it is funded

Through affiliate commissions. If you subscribe to a tool after following a link from this site, the vendor may pay us a percentage at no additional cost to you. We take no payment for placement, no sponsored articles, and no vendor review of drafts before publication. The affiliate disclosure lists the programmes we work with and what that means for you.

That funding model has an obvious bias risk, and the mitigation is procedural: every article includes a section on who should not buy the product, and at least one recommendation that pays us little or nothing. If you find an article where the only recommendation is the highest-commission option, write to us — that is a bug.

Corrections

Prices move. If something on this site no longer matches the vendor's page, open an issue on the public repository with the page and what you see. Substantive corrections are applied to the article with the date, rather than silently edited out.

Contact

The repository's issue tracker is the contact channel: github.com/whdgus1021-png/opsstack-guide/issues. It is public, which keeps corrections visible and means we do not ask readers for personal details to reach us.

Disclaimer

Nothing here is legal, tax or financial advice. Software pricing and licensing change without notice, and licence terms — especially the self-hosting terms of open-core products — must be read at the source before you depend on them commercially. We link to the primary documents wherever a claim depends on one.
