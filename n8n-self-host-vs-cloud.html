n8n self-hosted vs n8n Cloud for a five-person team

The server costs €5.49. The maintenance is what you are actually choosing between — so let us price it honestly, then look at the one licence line an agency must not cross.

Prices checked 22 Nov 2026 10 min read n8n · hosting

Short answer

For five people, Cloud Pro at €50/month (yearly) is the cheaper option unless someone on the team genuinely enjoys server upkeep — and Starter at €20 is cheaper still if none of your workflows takes longer than five minutes to run.

Self-host if you need something Cloud will not give you: custom nodes, bash and Python steps, credentials that must not leave your infrastructure, or more concurrency than Starter's five executions without paying €667 for Business.

Do not self-host to save money you have not counted in hours. Below, that comes out to roughly 1.5 hours a month before self-hosting loses to Pro.

On paper it looks absurd: €600 a year against €66. This is the calculation that convinces people to self-host, and it is missing exactly one line — the hours — plus a set of limits that decide whether the cheap Cloud plan is even usable.

Pricing the hours nobody invoices

A self-hosted n8n instance is a small production system. In a normal month it needs: Docker image and n8n version updates (they ship frequently), a database backup you have actually restored once, a certificate that renews itself, disk and memory checks, and an hour of attention when something breaks at the worst moment. For a five-person team that is realistically 1.5–3 hours a month, spiking on the month something goes wrong.

At €40 an hour, one hour of maintenance a month already makes self-hosting more expensive than Cloud Pro. That is the honest break-even: self-hosting wins only if you are below roughly 1.5 hours a month, or if you would not otherwise be billing those hours — a founder on a Sunday, a technical co-owner, a quiet week.

There is a second, subtler cost: risk concentration. A Cloud outage is an outage someone else is fixing, and it is on their status page. A self-hosted outage at 6pm on invoice day is yours, including the client conversation.

The limits that decide this before price does

Five-minute runs (Starter)

Cloud Starter caps a single execution at five minutes; Pro allows 40. Self-hosted has no cap beyond your own configuration. Any workflow that pages through a few thousand spreadsheet rows, generates a large PDF, or waits for a human approval will hit Starter's cap and fail mid-run. If that is your workload, Starter is not a cheaper Pro — it is a different product.

Five concurrent executions (Starter)

Concurrency is how many runs can be in flight at once. Five is fine for scheduled jobs and webhooks arriving one at a time. It is not fine if 40 client forms land in the same minute, or if an AI workflow sits waiting on a model response while others queue. Pro raises it to 20–50; self-hosted goes as far as your server, plus workers in queue mode.

Projects (1 on Starter, 3 on Pro)

Projects are the closest thing n8n has to per-client separation: credentials, workflows and members are scoped to a project. One project on Starter means the whole agency shares one workspace. Three on Pro maps to "internal", "client A", "client B" — enough for a small studio, not enough for twelve retainers. Business (€667/month) raises it to six, which is the point where most agencies stop and ask whether they need separate instances instead.

Logs and saved executions

Seven days of execution history on Starter vs 30 on Pro. When a client says "the report was wrong last month", seven days is not enough to reconstruct what happened. Self-hosted keeps whatever you configure, but you are the one storing and pruning it.

The licence line agencies must not cross

Read this before you host for a client

Self-hosted n8n ships under the Sustainable Use License, which grants use for your own internal business purposes. Running n8n as a service for other businesses — hosting a client's workflows on your instance, or reselling it as part of a managed service — is outside that grant and requires a commercial agreement with n8n. Hosting your own agency's automations is internal use; hosting your client's automations on the same box is where the line sits.

This matters more than it looks, because "we'll put each client in their own project" is the natural agency plan. If the client owns and operates the workflows, the cleanest structure is a Cloud account in the client's name with you as an invited user — they pay €20–50, you do the work, and nobody is running a hosting service on a licence that does not cover it. Confirm the current terms on n8n's licence page before you build a commercial arrangement on top of your reading of them.

If you do self-host, get these right

  • Back up the encryption key with the database. Credentials are encrypted with the instance key. Rebuild without it and every connection has to be re-authorised — the data is not gone, but your whole integration layer is.
  • Use Postgres, not the default SQLite, once more than one person depends on it. SQLite is fine for a test instance and becomes a liability with backup and concurrency.
  • Pin the image tag (a specific version rather than latest) and update deliberately, on a day you can watch it. Automatic upgrades on a production workflow engine are how Monday mornings get ruined.
  • Enable the provider's snapshot backup and then actually restore one. A backup you have never restored is a hope.
  • Put it behind TLS and do not expose the editor to the open internet without at least basic authentication and a firewall rule.

The sequence that works

Start on Cloud Starter for a month with two real client workflows. You will learn whether your slowest workflow fits in five minutes and how often you hit concurrency before you have paid for a server or committed to maintenance. Move to Pro, or to a self-hosted instance, with evidence in hand instead of a spreadsheet guess.

FAQ

Can an agency host n8n for its clients?

Under the Sustainable Use License, self-hosted n8n is licensed for your own internal business purposes. Hosting it as a service you sell, or running client-owned automations on your instance, is outside that grant and needs a commercial agreement with n8n.

How much server does five people need?

A 2 GB instance is the usual starting point — $12/month at DigitalOcean, or a Hetzner shared-vCPU server from €5.49/month excluding VAT. Move to 4 GB ($24) when you start paging through large sheets or running many executions at once.

What happens if I lose the encryption key?

Stored credentials cannot be decrypted. Nothing else is lost, but every integration has to be authorised again. Back the key up separately from the server snapshot, in a password manager or secrets store.

Is self-hosting really free?

The licence is free for internal use; the server is not. Realistically €66–288 a year for hosting, plus 1.5–3 hours a month of attention. Price the hours at what they would otherwise earn and the "free" usually becomes €50–130 a month.
