HubSpot Starter vs the free CRM, for agencies under ten

The free tier is excellent and includes two users. For an agency, that is the whole decision — everything else is arithmetic on seats.

Prices checked 22 Nov 2026 8 min read HubSpot · systeme.io

Short answer

Two people or fewer: the free CRM is genuinely enough, and paying for Starter buys you tidier branding more than capability.

Three to ten people: Starter, and the current new-customer offer changes the maths a lot — $7 a seat instead of $20. Take it with the annual commitment if you are sure of the tool; the discount is against a list price you will pay later.

Anything above that: stop and look at Professional's $1,300 monthly floor. For a ten-person studio that is $1,480 a month, and most agencies this size are buying a CRM they will use at 15% of its capacity.

What the free CRM actually includes

HubSpot's free CRM is not a trial. It includes contact and company records, deals, tasks, email templates and tracked email, forms, and the Smart CRM database — for up to two users. It has no time limit.

That limit is the important sentence. Not "two seats free, then a small fee", but a hard edge: a third person who needs to see the pipeline has to be on a paid seat. For a studio of five, the free tier is a two-person CRM wearing a five-person agency's name.

If your agency is one founder plus a contractor who never logs in, free is a real option and you should take it. If the person who answers the phone needs to log a call, you are already past it.

What Starter adds, and what it costs

Starter is sold per seat. The list price in HubSpot's own Product and Services Catalog is $20 per seat per month. As of November 2026 HubSpot is running a new-customer offer of up to 65% off: $7 per seat per month with an annual commitment, or $10 month-to-month.

What the paid seat buys is not more contacts — it is automation and the removal of limits: marketing email without HubSpot branding, more form and workflow behaviour, 1,000 marketing contacts on Marketing Hub Starter, and 500 HubSpot credits a month for the AI features. Whether any of that matters depends on what you were going to use the CRM for.

On that promotion

A 65% discount with an annual commitment is a good deal and a bad habit. It is priced to get a year of your data first, and the renewal will be at whatever the list price is then. If the agency needs HubSpot's reporting and email in one place, take the discount and budget the renewal at $20 a seat. If the answer to "why HubSpot?" is "it was free to start", take the discount anyway — but put the renewal date in the calendar today.

The Professional cliff

Between Starter and Professional there is no middle. Professional opens at $1,300/month billed annually with six seats included, and additional seats cost $45 each. For a ten-person agency, that is $1,480/month — 7.4x Starter's list price for the same ten people.

Professional buys real things: multi-touch attribution, custom reporting, sequences, teams and permissions, and the automation that HubSpot's marketing engine is actually famous for. None of those become useful because you have ten people. They become useful when you have a pipeline worth forecasting and someone whose job is to run it. Below that, the upgrade mostly buys a monthly invoice you have to justify.

Our rule of thumb: a HubSpot Professional licence should be paying for itself in attributed revenue within one quarter, or it is a subscription to a dashboard.

Who should not use HubSpot at all

  • Agencies tracking projects, not deals. HubSpot is a CRM with a project-shaped hole. If your daily question is "what stage is the Henderson website at", you want a project tool with a client list, not a deal pipeline where every client is one card.
  • Studios with five clients and no sales motion. Five relationships live perfectly well in a shared inbox and a spreadsheet. A CRM earns its keep when inbound volume exceeds what one person can hold in their head.
  • Anyone who will not maintain it. A CRM is a habit. Ten seats of HubSpot with stale deals is more expensive than a spreadsheet that people actually update.

If you do buy Starter, set it up like this

  1. Decide who gets a seat. Seats are the cost driver. Designers who only need to see a client's history do not need one; the person who owns inbound does.
  2. Clean the pipeline stages before importing. Import a messy spreadsheet and you will spend the first month fixing duplicates instead of using the tool. Five stages, no more.
  3. Turn on the two automations that matter: a task when a deal goes quiet for seven days, and a notification when a form comes in. Everything else can wait.
  4. Connect the inbox once, properly. Tracked email is most of the value at this tier, and it only works if everyone sends from the connected address.
  5. Put the renewal date and the list price in the budget now, so the second-year invoice is not a surprise.

FAQ

How many users does HubSpot's free CRM include?

Two. Above that you need paid seats, and a Starter Core Seat lists at $20 per seat per month — currently $7 with an annual commitment for new customers, or $10 month-to-month.

When does Professional make sense for an agency?

Rarely below fifteen people or a five-figure monthly retainer book. At $1,300/month plus $45 a seat beyond six, it needs a dedicated owner and attributed revenue to justify itself.

Is the $7 offer the real price?

It is a real price for new customers on an annual commitment, and a temporary one. The list price in HubSpot's own catalog is $20 per seat per month; plan for that at renewal.
