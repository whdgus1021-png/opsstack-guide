Disclosure

Affiliate disclosure

Clear, in plain English, because it is required — and because a review site that hides this is not worth reading.

Last updated 22 November 2026

The short version

OpsStack Guide earns money through affiliate commissions. If you click a link to a product on this site and then buy a subscription, the vendor may pay us a percentage of that subscription, usually for the first year and sometimes for the life of the account. You pay the same price you would pay without us. The commission comes out of the vendor's marketing budget, not your invoice.

Programmes we take part in

Where a link on this site is an affiliate link, it points to the vendor's own sign-up page with an identifier attached. Links to these vendors may carry that identifier:

  • Make (make.com)
  • systeme.io (systeme.io)
  • n8n (n8n.io)
  • Zapier (zapier.com)
  • HubSpot (hubspot.com)

If a product's programme does not accept us, or we have not joined it, the link is an ordinary link and we earn nothing. Links to documentation, licence texts and GitHub repositories are never affiliate links.

What that does not buy

  • No paid placement. Nobody can pay to appear in an article, to move up a table, or to be described as a "best buy".
  • No vendor approval of drafts. Vendors do not see articles before publication.
  • No suppression of bad findings. Every review includes the case against the product, including the vendor who pays the highest commission. Our method requires a "who should not buy this" section.
  • No sponsored posts. If that ever changes, the post will carry a separate, prominent sponsorship label — and it has not changed.

How you can tell

Outbound links to a vendor's sign-up or pricing page are marked rel="sponsored" in the page source, which is the machine-readable version of this disclosure. Article pages carry a short disclosure above the first section, not buried in a footer.

The rules we are following

This disclosure follows the US Federal Trade Commission's guidance on endorsements and testimonials (16 CFR Part 255), which requires a clear and conspicuous disclosure of material connections between a publisher and the products it recommends. Readers in the EU and UK have equivalent disclosure requirements under consumer protection rules; the same text applies.

What we do not do

We do not buy traffic, run pop-ups that hide content behind an email address, or use tracking pixels. There are no cookies on this site and no analytics. If that changes, this page will say so before it changes.
