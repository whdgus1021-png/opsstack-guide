Make vs Zapier vs n8n for a small agency

All three run the same automation. Only one of them charges you per step, and that difference is worth more than every feature on the comparison grid.

Prices checked 22 Nov 2026 12 min read Make · Zapier · n8n

The verdict in 60 seconds

Choose Make if you want a visual builder, multi-step workflows and unlimited teammates on the cheapest real plan. At $9/month (annual) for 10,000 credits it is the best value in this table by a wide margin, and the free tier is generous enough to evaluate properly.

Choose Zapier if the work depends on a long tail of app connectors, or if a non-technical account manager has to maintain the Zaps. You are paying around 27x more per unit of work for that, so budget for it: 2,000 tasks a month is $49, and multi-step workflows need that paid plan.

Choose n8n if you can run a server and want the price to stop scaling with volume. Executions are counted per run, not per step, so an 8-step workflow costs the same as a 2-step one. Self-hosted Community Edition is free; Cloud is €20–50/month.

Three tools, three billing units — and why it matters

The pricing pages look similar and are not. Make sells credits, and every module that runs inside a scenario burns at least one. Zapier sells tasks, and each successful action step burns one (the trigger itself does not). n8n sells executions, and one execution is one complete run of a workflow no matter how many nodes are inside it.

That last one is the structural difference. Suppose your client-onboarding workflow has six steps: a form submission, a CRM record, a project in your PM tool, a Slack message, a Drive folder, and an invoice draft.

Units consumed by one run of the same 6-step workflow
ToolUnits per runWhat gets counted
Make6 creditsEvery module that executes, trigger included
Zapier5–6 tasksEach successful action; the trigger is free
n8n1 executionThe whole run, regardless of node count

Counting rules are the vendors' own. Filters and paths that stop a run early consume fewer units on Make and Zapier; n8n still charges the execution once it starts.

This is why "Zapier is the expensive one" is not a meme, and also why it is not the whole story: a two-step Zap and an eight-step Zap cost Zapier wildly different amounts, while n8n does not care. The more of your process you automate in one flow, the better n8n's model reads — and the worse Zapier's does.

Two limits decide most agency decisions here. First, two-step Zaps on Free means the free tier cannot do onboarding, reporting or invoicing workflows at all — those need at least four steps. A three-person ops team that wants to see each other's Zaps is looking at $69 before it has automated anything.

The 5-minute execution ceiling on Starter is the single most under-read number on n8n's page. It is fine for webhooks and CRM syncs. It is not fine for a workflow that pages through a few thousand rows of a client's export, or one that waits for a human to approve a proposal before continuing — those hit the wall mid-run and fail, and the failure looks like a bug in your workflow rather than a plan limit.

Make sells volume. n8n sells the absence of a meter.

What actually breaks in an agency, not in a demo

Agencies break automation in four specific places, and the plan grid rarely shows any of them.

1. The 15-minute polling floor

Make's free plan runs scheduled scenarios no more often than every 15 minutes; Core drops that to one minute. If a client's contact form feeds a Slack alert that someone is supposed to answer within five minutes, the free plan is not a smaller version of the paid one — it is a different service level. Zapier's free tier polls on a similar cadence. n8n self-hosted has no floor other than your own server and the schedule you set.

2. The number of active workflows

Two active scenarios on Make Free is the limit that surprises most people, because 1,000 credits sounds like plenty while two workflows is one client's onboarding plus one internal sync. Every agency bundle — eight clients, four workflows each — starts at 32 active scenarios.

3. Seats, and who is allowed to touch it

This is where Make's pricing is quietly generous: unlimited users on every plan, Free included. Your account manager can look at a broken scenario without a seat purchase. n8n also gives unlimited users on all plans, split into projects (1 on Starter, 3 on Pro). Zapier's collaboration story starts at Team, $69/month for 25 users.

4. The one long-running job

Any workflow that waits — for approval, for a document, for a nightly batch — runs into execution-time caps: 5 minutes on Make Free and n8n Starter, 40 minutes on Make Core and above and n8n Pro. Before you choose, find your slowest real workflow and check it against that number; it is the most common reason a pilot that worked becomes "unreliable" in production.

The mistake we see most

Buying Zapier Professional because "we already know Zapier", then rebuilding the same workflow in Make eighteen months later when the task bill passes $150/month. If your automation runs on a schedule and touches systems your own team controls, Zapier's connector depth is not earning that premium. If it runs on ad-hoc triggers from a dozen niche SaaS tools, it is.

Who should not buy Make

If the person maintaining the automations is a designer or an account manager with no patience for a canvas, Make's model — bundles, iterators, and module-by-module data mapping — will generate support tickets. Zapier's linear step list is objectively easier to hand over. Also skip Make if you need a specific niche connector: by Make's own count it connects over 1,000 third-party apps, against Zapier's 9,000+.

Who should not buy Zapier

Anyone whose workflows are scheduled and whose volume grows with the number of clients. A per-step meter on a client-count business means your software bill rises every time you win work, and the price never comes back down when a client leaves. Also: don't buy Professional expecting team access; that is Team, at three times the price.

Who should not buy n8n

Teams without anyone who can keep a Linux box patched, or who would treat "the server is down" as an emergency with no owner. Self-hosting is genuinely cheap and genuinely a maintenance obligation: updates, database backups, TLS certificates, and a monitoring check you trust. If nobody owns that, Cloud Starter's €20 is the honest price of not owning it — and if your slowest workflow exceeds five minutes, Cloud Pro's €50 is the real comparison, not Starter.

What we would do with a $50/month budget

Make Core at $9 as the workhorse, and put the remaining $41 toward the tools the automations connect to rather than a second automation engine. Add n8n Community on a $12 droplet only when a specific workflow needs custom code, long-running waits, or data that cannot leave your own infrastructure — and keep the two systems apart by giving each a clear job, so nobody has to remember which tool owns which workflow.

Then revisit in a quarter. Make repackaged its billing unit in August 2026, Zapier keeps moving features between tiers, and n8n has been raising Cloud prices as it adds AI features. Any of those can change the arithmetic faster than your usage changes.

FAQ

Is Make cheaper than Zapier?

Per unit of work, yes, by roughly 27x at comparable tiers: $0.90 per 1,000 credits on Make Core against $24.50 per 1,000 tasks on Zapier Professional at its 2,000-task tier ($49/month, yearly billing). The units are not identical — Make counts the trigger module, Zapier does not — but no reasonable reading closes a gap that size.

Do I need a paid Zapier plan for multi-step Zaps?

Yes. Free is 100 tasks/month and two-step Zaps only. Multi-step, webhooks, filters, paths and premium apps all begin on Professional.

Can n8n run on a cheap VPS?

Yes. A $12/month 2 GB instance, or a Hetzner shared-vCPU server from €5.49/month, handles dozens of workflows for a small team in default mode. Heavy concurrency (queue mode) is a Business-plan feature, so plan the ceiling before you promise a client 24/7 volume.

Can one account hold several clients' automations?

Technically all three can. Make has unlimited users but no per-client separation beyond folders and teams; n8n's projects (1 on Starter, 3 on Pro) map cleanly to clients; Zapier's folders do the same above Team. If you need per-client logins and invoicing, you are really asking for separate workspaces, which is a different and more expensive question.
