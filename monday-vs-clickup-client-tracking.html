monday.com vs ClickUp for tracking client projects

One vendor charges for three seats before you start and bills client guests four to a seat until Pro. The other has no seat floor and hands out guest seats by the dozen, then puts dashboards behind Business. The bill flips with the number of clients you carry.

Prices checked 24 Nov 2026 8 min read monday.com · ClickUp

Short answer

For a three-to-ten-person agency showing clients their work, ClickUp Business at $17 per user per month billed yearly is the easier bill to defend: no seat floor, dashboards included, thirty client guest seats for a five-person team.

monday.com wins when the work is board-shaped and the client wants a timeline: Standard at $12 a seat includes shareable boards and Gantt. But guests consume seats from the fourth, so Pro at $19 is usually the real comparison rather than Standard.

Client access is what changes the bill

Agencies buy these tools so a client can open a link and see where the retainer stands. That is a guest in both products — handled very differently.

monday.com: three guests, then a quarter of a seat each

On Standard, guests are free to three. From the fourth, they are counted in groups of four against paid seats: four to seven guests use one seat, eight to eleven use two. Guests need a different email domain from the account creator — fine for clients, awkward for freelancers on your own domain. Separately, unlimited free viewers from Basic upward give read-only access that never touches the bill.

ClickUp: unlimited read-only, a fixed number of clients who can comment

View-only guests are unlimited on every paid plan. Guests who comment or edit need a permission-controlled seat, and the allowance scales with the team: Core gives five for a one-person Workspace plus two per additional paid user, Business ten plus five. Five people on Business therefore hold thirty. One trap: on Free Forever, guests are unrestricted editors whose permissions cannot be changed.

Where the plan actually changes

On monday.com's Standard plan the fourth client guest is where the bill starts moving. Carry five or more clients at two contacts each and the guest seats cost about the gap to Pro, where guests are unlimited. Price Pro before committing to Standard.

Two structural differences sit underneath. monday.com's three-seat floor means a two-person studio pays for three seats; ClickUp bills per user, so two people on Core is $18 a month. And every ClickUp member must be on one plan, so each client Workspace is a second subscription.

Where they differ once you are inside

Automations. monday.com has none on Free or Basic; Standard gives 250 automation and 250 integration actions a month, and its own help centre warns that allowance is usually gone within days once a recipe notifies several people or maps several columns. Pro multiplies it by a hundred. For client tracking — status emails, deadline moves, form submissions — that is the limit that pushes Standard buyers to Pro. ClickUp counts actions per Workspace: 1,000 on Core with 500 active rules, 10,000 on Business with unlimited rules. Webhook, email and SMS automations start at Business.

Reporting and history. monday.com lets dashboards span several boards from Standard, and Pro adds private boards and private docs — the feature that keeps a rate card off a client-visible board. ClickUp includes dashboards from Business (a trial on Core), alongside private views and private docs. ClickUp's activity view keeps seven days on Core and is unlimited only from Business; monday.com keeps a six-month activity log on Standard and a year on Pro. When a client disputes what was agreed in a comment, that history is worth more than the price gap.

API calls. monday.com Standard allows 1,000 a day, Pro 10,000; ClickUp's self-serve plans allow 100 a minute. If you pull project data into Looker Studio every morning, monday's daily ceiling arrives sooner than expected across ten boards.

Who should not buy this

Not monday.com if you are two people and the seat floor stings, if you expect automations on the cheapest paid plan (Basic has none), or if more than three clients need guest access on Standard — the fourth starts eating seats. Not if your reporting build makes many API calls a day on Standard.

Not ClickUp if client reporting matters on a cheap plan: dashboards and timesheets are Business features, so Core is a decoy for that agency. Not if clients must edit in volume — past thirteen guests on a five-person Core Workspace the next becomes a paid seat. Not if you need audit trails, SCIM, custom roles or branded forms; those are Enterprise.

Not either if you run under ten active projects and the client only wants a monthly PDF. A shared sheet and a scheduled report will do for a while — our client reporting build covers that route, including the free-tier trap that leaves dashboards a week behind.

The sequence that works

Run both free plans on the same two client projects for a fortnight, inviting one client as a viewer. You will learn whether the team thinks in boards or spaces and how many automations a normal week burns — before either vendor has your card.

FAQ

Does monday.com have a minimum number of seats?

Yes, three on every paid plan. Free allows two; above that the seat selector starts at three, so a two-person studio pays for three. ClickUp publishes no seat minimum and bills per paid user, though the whole Workspace moves at once.

How many client guests can I add for free?

monday.com Standard includes three, then bills every four guests as one seat, so ten guests cost two seats; Pro and Enterprise allow unlimited. ClickUp gives unlimited view-only guests on paid plans, plus five permission-controlled seats and two per additional user on Core, ten and five on Business.

Which is cheaper for five people and ten client users?

Almost a tie on the plans built for client access: monday.com Standard with seven seats is $84 a month billed yearly, ClickUp Business with five seats and thirty guest seats is $85. ClickUp Core at $45 is half the price, but dashboards are trial-only there and timesheets start at Business.

Can I track client projects on monday.com Basic?

Not really. Automations and integrations start on Standard, and shareable boards and guests are not part of Basic; it adds unlimited viewers, unlimited items and 5 GB of storage over Free. Client-facing work needs Standard or Pro.

Which is better for client reporting?

monday.com's multi-board dashboards start at Standard. ClickUp lists dashboards as a trial on Free and Core and includes them from Business, so multi-client reporting means Business at $17 per user per month billed yearly.
