Make alternatives for agencies that need client sub-accounts

Make does not have client sub-accounts. It has teams, and on Core and Pro you get one. Four tools that do separate clients — priced at eight clients on 28 November 2026.

Short answer

Make has no client sub-accounts. Its unit is the organisation — one plan, one bill, one credit pool — and teams, which hold the connections and scenarios, are capped at one until you reach the Teams plan at $29 a month. For eight clients, expect $72 as eight Core organisations, $17 on systeme.io, $297 on GoHighLevel, or eight self-hosted n8n instances at about $48.

Does Make have client sub-accounts?

No. Make has organisations and teams, and neither is a client sub-account. An organisation is the container that carries the pricing plan and the billing; a team is where the scenarios, connections, webhooks, data stores and keys live. The distinction matters because only one of those two can be multiplied cheaply, and it is not the one that carries authority over a client's data.

Make documents the rule plainly: each organisation has its own pricing plan and billing, and any user can create another organisation even while a member of the first. Teams are different. Scenarios, connections, templates, webhooks, keys, devices, data stores, data structures and custom functions all belong to exactly one team, and you cannot move them afterwards. On any plan below Teams, Make allows one team per organisation and no role management inside it.

So a five-person studio on Make Core — the $9 plan most small agencies buy — has exactly one place to put client automations. Every client's Google Ads connection, every webhook, every data store sits in that one team, visible to every user in the organisation. Make's own comparison table lists "Teams and team roles" as not included on Free, Core and Pro, and included from Teams upward.

The team limits and the ownership rules come from Make's help pages on organisations and teams. Free allows two active scenarios, a 15-minute minimum interval and a 5-minute run cap; every paid plan allows unlimited active scenarios, one-minute scheduling and 40-minute runs.

Two details in that table decide most agency decisions. The first is that credits do not divide by team: 10,000 credits are bought by the organisation, and a heavy client eats the pool every other client draws from. Per-team credit ceilings exist only on Enterprise, which is quoted, not listed. The second is that moving is one-way: because a scenario or connection belongs to exactly one team, re-organising clients later means rebuilding the connections, not dragging a folder.

Which alternatives give an agency real client sub-accounts?

Two of the five tools sell true sub-accounts — systeme.io and GoHighLevel — and both are marketing platforms with an automation layer, not automation engines. n8n sells projects, capped by plan. Zapier sells seats and folders on Team, and calls its separation Workspaces only on Enterprise. Make sells organisations. The table below compares what each one meters, how many client spaces you get, and what eight clients would cost.

What systeme.io's unlimited sub-accounts actually buy

Every systeme.io plan, including the free one, lets you create unlimited sub-accounts, and each sub-account carries your plan's full limits at no extra cost. The company states it in one sentence on the pricing page: if your plan allows 50 funnels, every sub-account you create can also have 50 funnels. Startup at $17 adds 5,000 contacts, 10 funnels and 5 courses; Webinar at $47 raises that to 10,000 contacts, 50 funnels and 20 courses; Unlimited at $97 removes the ceilings.

What it does not buy is Make's connector library. systeme.io automates inside its own stack — funnels, email campaigns, automation rules, workflows, courses and bookings — and the free plan allows one automation rule. If the client work is "deliver their funnel, email sequence and course and let them log in", $17 for unlimited accounts is the cheapest separation on this page and not close. If the client work is "sync their Shopify orders into their accounting sheet every hour", systeme.io is the wrong category of tool, unlimited sub-accounts or not.

What GoHighLevel's sub-accounts buy

GoHighLevel charges by agency plan, not by client, once you reach Agency Unlimited: $297 a month covers unlimited sub-accounts, unlimited contacts and unlimited users. Agency Starter is $97 a month for three sub-accounts — $32.33 each, which is the most expensive per-client line here — and the $497 Agency Pro plan adds SaaS mode, the ability to sell subscriptions on your own site with usage rebilled to clients.

The catch is usage. SMS, email sends, phone numbers and AI features are billed on top, and the vendor's own add-on list runs to things like $50 a month per sub-account for an AI employee plan or $10 a month per sub-account for the WhatsApp integration. For an agency whose clients actually send messages, the $297 is the floor, not the bill.

Why n8n's projects rarely reach eight clients

n8n gives you one shared project on Starter, three on Pro and six on Business, so eight clients cannot each have a project on any self-serve plan. Pro at €50 is $54.79 a month for three projects — $18.26 a project — and Business at €667 is self-hosted with six, $121.79 a project. Unlimited projects start at Enterprise, which is quoted. Projects also isolate access, not infrastructure: every project on an instance shares one deployment and database.

Self-hosting inverts the arithmetic. n8n's Community edition is free and, per n8n's own documentation, the one thing it lacks that matters here is team projects — which a single-client instance does not need. Eight small servers on Hetzner's shared cloud start at €5.49 each, about $48 a month in total. You trade the subscription for running eight installs, their upgrades and their backups, which is the subject of our n8n self-hosted vs Cloud breakdown.

What do eight clients actually cost on each?

Eight clients cost $17 a month on systeme.io Startup, $29 on one Make Teams account, $72 as eight Make Core organisations, $297 on GoHighLevel Unlimited and $69 on Zapier Team — per client that is $2.13, $3.63, $9.00, $37.13 and $8.63. Divide the plan by the client count and the ranking changes completely from the sticker prices, which is why the per-client figure is the only one worth putting in a proposal.

The trap in the cheap option

Per-client cost falls as clients are added on GoHighLevel and systeme.io, and rises on Make's organisation model, because eight organisations is eight subscriptions. That is the trade: Make's $72 buys eight separate credit pools and eight separate bills, which is what you want when a client pays for their own automation. The $29 Teams plan buys the same visual separation with a shared credit pool and no per-team ceiling, so one runaway scenario pauses everybody's reporting.

Where does the sub-account model break?

It breaks at three points: shared metering, client access, and what happens when the client leaves. Each platform fails differently, and the failure usually shows up in month three rather than at signup.

1. Shared metering. On Make Teams, Zapier Team and n8n Cloud, one pool of credits, tasks or executions serves every client. A batch import or a misconfigured polling loop takes everyone offline at once. Make's per-team limits and n8n's per-project separation of executions do not change the billing pool; only separate organisations, separate instances or a per-client subscription do. If a client's contract has an uptime clause, this is the line that decides the architecture.

2. Client access. A sub-account is something a client can sign into; a team or a project is something your staff can be kept out of. GoHighLevel can white-label the desktop app so the client sees your brand, and systeme.io's sub-accounts are full accounts with their own logins. Make's unlimited users mean you can invite a client into their own team on the Teams plan, but the interface carries Make's branding and your other teams are one permissions mistake away.

3. The exit. When a client leaves, the deliverable is often the automation itself. On systeme.io and GoHighLevel a sub-account can be transferred or dismantled as a unit. On Make, a scenario belongs to one team and cannot be moved, so handing a client their own automations means rebuilding connections inside their organisation. Plan for that hour before you sign, not on the last day.

Who should not buy this

Not systeme.io if the work is integration-led rather than funnel-led. Its unlimited sub-accounts are the best value here, and its connector set is a fraction of Make's 3,500 apps. Also not it if a client's automation needs more than a handful of rules on the free plan — one automation rule is the whole free allowance.

Not GoHighLevel if you are under five clients. $97 for three sub-accounts is $32.33 a client before any SMS bill, and the platform rewards agencies that sell marketing services at volume, not studios automating a spreadsheet pipeline. Also not it if nobody on the team wants to learn an all-in-one CRM, funnels, calendars and reputation stack to get an automation engine.

Not n8n Cloud if client separation is the requirement. Starter's one project and Pro's three are too few for an agency, and the jump to six is €667 self-hosted. Not Zapier Team if a client is ever supposed to see their own automations: folders are not a boundary, and Workspaces sit behind an Enterprise contract.

Not Make Teams if clients pay their own automation bills, or if one client's volume can starve the others. Pay the $9 per organisation instead, and accept eight subscriptions to cancel rather than one.

Not any of these if the answer is a spreadsheet and a scheduled email. Our client reporting build runs that way for a single client at no cost, and the trap in it is the free tier, not the tool count.

How to decide in one afternoon

Start from who must be logged in, then from who pays, and only then from the price. Those three answers pick the tool faster than any feature comparison, because they eliminate whole categories.

  • Clients log in and see their own data: systeme.io if the work is funnels and email, GoHighLevel if it is CRM, calls, SMS and reputation. Both sell accounts, not projects.
  • Only your staff log in, and clients pay their own automation: one Make organisation per client on Core at $9, or free while the client stays under two active scenarios and 15-minute polling.
  • Only your staff log in, and you absorb the cost: Make Teams at $29 for teams and roles, with eyes open about the shared 10,000 credits.
  • You want the engine under your own control: n8n self-hosted, one instance per client, about $6 a month of server each plus the maintenance. The self-host vs Cloud article prices the hours.
  • You are already on Zapier and the Zaps are simple: Team at $69 for shared folders and connections, and keep client data out of shared Zaps. Our Zapier alternatives guide covers the migration routes when the task bill is the problem instead.

Whichever route you take, fix the ownership rule before the first client: which organisation, team or project a new client's connections go into, and who can see them. Make's one-team limit on Core means that rule has to be written down, because the platform will not enforce it for you.

FAQ

Does Make have client sub-accounts?

No. Make's container is the organisation — one plan, one billing relationship, one credit pool — and teams inside it hold the scenarios and connections. Plans below Teams allow a single team, so Core and Pro cannot separate one client's connections from another's. Teams at $29 a month allows several teams with team roles.

What is the cheapest way to separate eight clients on Make?

Eight Core organisations at $9 each, $72 a month on annual billing, with a separate credit pool and bill for every client. One Teams plan at $29 is cheaper but shares 10,000 credits and offers per-team credit ceilings only on Enterprise.

Which alternatives actually give unlimited client sub-accounts?

systeme.io, on every plan including the free one, with each sub-account carrying the parent plan's limits. GoHighLevel gives three on Agency Starter at $97 and unlimited on Agency Unlimited at $297. n8n caps projects at one, three and six by plan. Zapier has no sub-accounts below Enterprise, where they are called Workspaces.

Is a client sub-account the same as a client login?

Not always. systeme.io and GoHighLevel sub-accounts are accounts a client can sign into. Make teams and n8n projects separate data and credentials for your staff; a client sees them only if you create a user with access to that team or project.

Can I run one n8n instance per client instead of paying for projects?

Yes. The Community edition is free to self-host and lacks only team projects, which a single-client instance does not need. Eight small servers start around €5.49 each a month, roughly $48 in total, and you pay in maintenance rather than subscription.
