Privacy

Privacy

This site does not ask who you are. Here is exactly what does get recorded, by whom, and why.

Effective 22 November 2026

What we do not collect

OpsStack Guide has no cookies, no analytics, no tracking pixels, no advertising tags, no newsletter form and no user accounts. There is no comment system. Nothing on this site sets a cookie of its own, and nothing profiles you across pages.

There is one small piece of JavaScript on the site in total: none. Pages are static HTML and CSS.

Hosting logs

The site is served by GitHub Pages. GitHub records technical information about requests to its infrastructure — typically IP address, user agent, and the time of the request — as part of operating the service and defending it against abuse. That processing is GitHub's, governed by the GitHub Privacy Statement, and we do not receive those logs in a form that identifies readers.

Affiliate links

When you click a link to a vendor's site, you leave this site and the vendor's own privacy policy applies. If the link carries an affiliate identifier, the vendor records that the visit came through this site — usually as a cookie on the vendor's domain, which is how the commission is attributed. That cookie belongs to the vendor, not to us. What we receive is aggregate: a count of referrals and conversions, not who you are.

Links to a vendor are marked rel="sponsored". See the affiliate disclosure for the full list.

Files you download

The spreadsheet template linked from the client reporting guide is a static CSV file. Downloading it tells us nothing; there is no download counter and no script on the link.

If you contact us

The contact channel is a public GitHub issue tracker. Anything you post there is public and is stored by GitHub under its terms. Please do not put personal information in an issue; if something requires privacy, say so without details and we will find another way.

Your rights

Because we hold no personal data about readers, there is nothing for us to export or erase. If you believe we do hold something — for example, an email you sent to a personal address — ask in the issue tracker and it will be addressed. Under the GDPR and similar regimes you have the right to access, correct and erase personal data; where the data is held by GitHub or a vendor, the request goes to them.

Changes

If this site ever adds analytics, a newsletter or comments, this page will describe it before the feature goes live, and the date at the top will change.
